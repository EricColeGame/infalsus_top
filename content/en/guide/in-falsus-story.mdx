## What Is the In Falsus Story? A Near-Future World Beneath the Shroud

The In Falsus story opens with an image that refuses to leave you alone: a long shadow falling across the world, and everyone beneath it quietly collared. Under an unseen gaze, no emotion and no falsehood goes unnoticed. It is a premise built on observation, truth, and the quiet terror of being fully known.

That premise matters because it is not decoration. The In Falsus story is woven directly into the rhythm gameplay rather than bolted on as an intermission between songs. Every encounter, every card you craft, and every note you clear feeds back into the narrative, which is why players who skim the dialogue often find themselves confused about why their options changed.

The game follows five high school girls in a near-future setting whose ordinary routines are interrupted when they are invited into a strange program — and then pushed toward an undiscovered side of their own reality. If you want the short version: five separate lives, one shared descent, and a world that punishes lies. You can see the full cast and premise on the [official In Falsus website](https://infalsus.lowiro.com/).

### The Core Premise in Plain Terms

| Story Element | What It Means |
|---|---|
| The Shroud | An enigmatic presence hanging over the world, tied to the theme of truth being bound and monitored |
| The Unseen Gaze | Nothing hidden survives it — emotion and deception are both visible |
| The Program | The invitation that pulls all five girls off their expected paths |
| The Path | Each girl is set toward her own heart, the hearts of others, and the heart of the world |
| World 01 | The first narrative world, described as bound to truth beneath the Shroud |

That table is essentially the skeleton of the entire plot. Everything else — the songs, the cards, the escalating threats in the Scenario — hangs off it.

## Meet the Five Protagonists Carrying the In Falsus Story

Each heroine is sixteen, enrolled at a different institution, and introduced through a short profile that reads like an observer's field notes rather than a friendly biography. That framing is deliberate: someone is watching these girls before the player ever meets them.

| Character | School | Personality Snapshot | Voice Actor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nia Cana | Akie High School | Anxious, ordinary, avoids thinking about the future; lives with her parents | Fuka Izumi |
| Mei Alder | Akie High School | Quiet, disinterested, frustrated; supports herself alone in a two-story maisonette | Ran Haruka |
| Ayame Takatsuki | The Amachi Institute of Art & Design | Sociable, careful, an awarded painter surrounded by friends | Kanon Amane |
| Iris Leal | Oskar Beck High School | Charming and violent; lives with her parents and younger sister | Ayame Fujikami |
| Story Étienne | Xenia Academy | A transferred student who chose to become mute; a voracious reader | Mirii Saito |

### Why the Profiles Feel Like Case Files

Notice the recurring note in the official character descriptions: "Friendless." It appears for Nia, Mei, and Iris, with a pointed addendum for Iris — she was not always friendless. Ayame is the outlier, socially surrounded yet described as "a bother" and "too clever, too careful."

For players tracking the In Falsus story, those details are not filler. They establish five girls who are isolated in different ways, which makes the program's offer land differently for each of them. One is desperate, one is indifferent, one is guarded by popularity, one is guarded by violence, and one has stopped speaking altogether.

## How the In Falsus Story Is Told Through Rhythm Gameplay

This is where In Falsus separates itself from a pure visual novel. The narrative is not a cutscene you sit through; it is a system you interact with. The official description frames it clearly: on the protagonists' journey, characters cross "soul to soul" with others — and sometimes with themselves — and those encounters become playable confrontations.

| System | What It Does | How It Serves the Story |
|---|---|---|
| Rhythm encounters | You brace against barrages of incoming notes across a sweeping aerial Field | Represents emotional and psychological clashes between characters |
| Four difficulty tiers | Guide you from first clears toward flow and mastery | Lets you choose how intense a story beat feels |
| Iota | The core material collected by clearing songs | The raw resource the narrative hands you for progressing |
| Blueprint grids | Iota is slotted into grid layouts | Grid completion determines the shape of your build |
| Cards | Crafted from completed Blueprints | Card effects vary based on how you filled the grid |
| Integration | Expanded as you earn rewards | Broadens your available approaches over time |
| Scenario | Where crafted Cards are used against stronger threats | The mode where story stakes and difficulty escalate together |

The takeaway: your Blueprint decisions are narrative decisions. Two players can clear the same song and walk away with different Cards, different advantages, and therefore different ways of surviving the next Scenario encounter.

### Reading the Four Difficulties as Pacing

If you are here primarily for the In Falsus story and not for leaderboard chasing, the four difficulty tiers function as a pacing dial. Lower tiers let you absorb dialogue-heavy chapters without fighting the chart. Higher tiers turn the same narrative beat into a test of endurance. Neither approach is wrong — but the game clearly expects you to move upward as the Scenario threats grow.

## The Structure of the In Falsus Story: Perspectives, Worlds, and Chapters

Rather than following one protagonist, In Falsus rotates between five separate viewpoints. The official material describes the game as following "the separate perspectives of 5 girls," and it labels its first narrative block as World 01, subtitled as a world bound to truth beneath the Shroud.

| Structural Element | Description |
|---|---|
| World | The top-level narrative container; World 01 is the starting block |
| Perspective | Which of the five girls you are following at a given moment |
| Scenario | The progression mode where Cards meet escalating opposition |
| Song Clear | The gate that yields Iota and moves you forward |
| Blueprint / Card | Your personalized build, shaped by grid decisions |

### Practical Implications of a Multi-Perspective Story

- **Track names carefully.** With five narrators, a character mentioned in one perspective may be the protagonist of another.
- **Do not assume linear chronology.** Separate perspectives often overlap in time.
- **Watch the "soul to soul" encounters.** When two girls meet in a rhythm sequence, that scene is usually the payoff for earlier setup.
- **Expect the program to be explained slowly.** The premise is deliberately coy about who is running it and why.

Community reports suggest most songs are unlocked through story progression rather than being available from the start, which means the In Falsus story and your song library are effectively the same thing. ## Player Experience: What the Community Says About the In Falsus Story

The most active debate in the game's community discussions is not about charts or scoring — it is about whether the story should be skippable. Player experience on the Steam forums breaks into two clear camps, and both sides have reasonable points.

| Position | The Argument |
|---|---|
| Story is optional | Players who came for the rhythm game want a true skip function, not just a text speed-up; they point out that other rhythm titles with heavy dialogue allow scene skipping |
| Story is the point | Others note the game was positioned as a hybrid visual novel experience, so removing the narrative removes the product |
| Middle ground | Several players suggest free play or practice modes as the answer, but others counter that most songs are gated behind story progress |

One frequently repeated observation is that the "skip" option behaves more like a speed-up than a true skip, which frustrates players who want to jump straight into the music. Another recurring theme: the RPG-style mechanics — Iota, Blueprints, Cards, and Integration — are widely considered the most interesting systems in the game, and some players want to engage with those systems while minimizing dialogue.

If you are deciding where you land, the honest answer is that In Falsus was built as a story-first experience with rhythm as its delivery mechanism. Treating the narrative as disposable means treating the Card system as unmotivated, because the two are designed as one loop.

## FAQ

**Do I need to play the In Falsus story to enjoy the rhythm gameplay?**
Community reports indicate most songs unlock through narrative progression, so yes — even players focused purely on charts will move through the story to build a library. Free play exists, but the full song list is tied to progression.

**Who are the five protagonists of the In Falsus story?**
Nia Cana, Mei Alder, Ayame Takatsuki, Iris Leal, and Story Étienne. All are sixteen, all attend different schools, and each is introduced through an observer-style profile that emphasizes how isolated she is.

**What are Iota and Blueprints in the In Falsus story?**
Iota is the core material you collect by clearing songs. You slot Iota into Blueprint grids, and how you complete a grid determines the effects of the Card it produces. Those Cards are then used during Scenario gameplay.

**Is the In Falsus story skippable?**
Player experience on the Steam forums suggests the current skip option accelerates text rather than skipping scenes outright, which remains the most debated feature in the community. If story is not what you want from the game, that debate is worth reading before you buy.

The In Falsus story rewards patience more than speed. Five isolated girls, one watching world, and a rhythm system that turns every emotional collision into something you have to play through — that combination is the reason the narrative sticks with people long after the last note.
