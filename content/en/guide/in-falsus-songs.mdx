## Why the In Falsus Song List Is the Real Progression System

In Falsus songs are the backbone of one of the most talked-about rhythm games on Steam, and the tracklist is the fastest way to understand how the game actually works. Knowing which In Falsus songs belong to the demo, which belong to the launch arc structure, and which difficulty tiers each chart supports turns a confusing library into a clear practice plan. This guide breaks down the tracklist, the chart levels, and the habits that help players climb faster — whether you are brand new or already chasing full clears.

Most rhythm games treat a song list as a menu. In Falsus treats it as a curriculum. Every track carries multiple chart tiers, and each tier is rated on the same numeric scale the community uses to compare difficulty across the whole library. That means your library is also your difficulty ladder: the song you can barely survive on one tier is often the same song you can farm for accuracy on another.

Here is the at-a-glance picture of how the library is built, based on official posts and community-maintained wikis.

| Element | What it means |
|---|---|
| Chart tiers per song | Four: MIN, EVO, ULT, FBD |
| Difficulty rating scale | Roughly 1–15, applied per chart, not per song |
| Launch library | Around 70 songs, with 40+ originals (community reports) |
| Library with DLC counted | Roughly 75 tracks once the five Virtual Singers DLC songs are included |
| Platform | Steam |
| Organization | Arcs and packs, plus unlock pools referenced by community trackers |

The practical consequence is simple: when players say they are "stuck," they usually are not stuck on a song. They are stuck on a tier. Separating those two ideas is the single biggest mindset shift this game rewards.

## MIN, EVO, ULT, FBD: How In Falsus Songs Chart Levels Work

Difficulty in In Falsus is layered. Each song carries four charts, and the community refers to them by tier name rather than by number alone. The numeric rating tells you where a chart sits on the global scale; the tier name tells you what kind of chart it is meant to be.

| Tier | Role | Typical rating range | When to attempt it |
|---|---|---|---|
| MIN | Entry point | Low end of the scale | First contact with a new song |
| EVO | Intermediate | Mid range | After a comfortable MIN clear |
| ULT | Advanced | Upper-mid range | Once pattern reading feels automatic |
| FBD | Top difficulty | Highest range | Endgame; expect stamina and precision demands |

A good illustration is "Be There" by PSYQUI, which community wikis list at 3 / 6 / 10 / 12 across the four tiers. That spread is normal, and it is why two players can both say they "cleared Be There" and mean completely different things.

Two rules of thumb follow from this. First, never judge a song by its easiest chart — a gentle MIN can hide a brutal FBD. Second, treat the rating as a ranking tool, not a promise: a 10 that suits your playstyle can be easier than a 9 that does not. Ratings describe difficulty in general, not difficulty for you specifically.

## Standout Artists, Unlock Tips, and Practice Habits

The artist roster is one of the strongest arguments for the library. Names like Camellia, ak+q, Feryquitous, aran, PSYQUI, and Qlarabelle appear across the tracklist, and the game leans heavily on original compositions rather than licensed filler — community counts put originals at more than half the launch library.

That matters for practice, because original charts are usually designed with the game's own mechanics in mind rather than retrofitted from another format.

Here are habits that consistently show up in player experience reports from people climbing the tiers:

- **Clear MIN on everything first.** A wide, shallow library beats a narrow, deep one early on. Variety teaches pattern vocabulary faster than grinding one chart.
- **Use EVO as your working tier.** It is the tier where most players actually improve, because it is hard enough to demand reading skill but forgiving enough to finish.
- **Replay for accuracy, not just for clears.** A cleared chart with sloppy timing is a chart you have not really learned.
- **Save FBD attempts for when you are fresh.** Top-tier charts punish fatigue far more than mid-tier ones do.
- **Track your own ratings, not just the game's.** Note which numbers you can clear consistently and which ones you cannot.

| Goal | Best tier to spend time on | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Building pattern vocabulary | MIN | Low pressure, high variety |
| Improving accuracy | EVO | Demanding but finishable |
| Raising your ceiling | ULT | Forces uncomfortable reading |
| Testing your limits | FBD | Reveals stamina and precision gaps |

A short, focused session beats a long, sloppy one almost every time. Twenty minutes on a tier just above your comfort zone will do more for you than an hour of re-clearing charts you already own.
