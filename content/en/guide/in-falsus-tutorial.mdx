In Falsus is a hybrid rhythm game, card battler, and visual novel, and its opening hour throws all three systems at you at once. That is exactly why a solid In Falsus tutorial matters: miss how note judgments feed card damage, or how Blueprint placement changes your stats, and your first real Encounter will punish you for it. What follows is the In Falsus tutorial I wish existed on day one — a plain-English walkthrough of the controls, the note types, the Card Integration loop, and the Scenario Timeline.

## What Kind of Game Is In Falsus?

In Falsus comes from lowiro and blends three systems that usually live in separate games: a rhythm game, a card battler, and an ADV-style visual novel. You play charts on 4 to 6+ keys, build Cards from Iota placed on a hex Blueprint, and read story scenes that unlock songs and Cards as you go.

| Pillar | What you actually do | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| Rhythm charts | Hit TAP, HOLD, FIELD, and FLICK notes in time with a song | Every song and every Encounter |
| Card Integration | Place Iota on a Blueprint, choose Traits, equip one Card per Phase | Loadout Select and card battles |
| ADV Scenario | Read first-person story scenes and select nodes on the Timeline | Scenario mode |

The three systems feed each other constantly. Story progress unlocks songs and Cards, your Cards decide how hard your notes hit during an Encounter, and your note accuracy decides whether those Cards actually win the fight. A useful In Falsus tutorial therefore has to cover all three pillars, which is what the rest of this guide does.

## Your First 30 Minutes: A Step-by-Step In Falsus Tutorial

The opening hour is heavily scripted, and almost every prompt teaches something you will reuse for the rest of the game. Based on community reports from the demo build, here is the order things arrive.

| # | What the game asks | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read or fast-forward the opening narration | Sets the tone; every scene can be re-read later |
| 2 | Choose "Prioritize Scenario" or "Challenge Rhythm Game" | Changes tutorial order only — it locks nothing |
| 3 | Learn the four ADV buttons | Back, auto-play, fast-forward, and log |
| 4 | Open the Hub's SCENARIO panel | Shows your arc, POV character, and in-world date |
| 5 | Open the Scenario Timeline | One colored line per character, nodes stamped by day and time of day |
| 6 | Clear the first Arc's nodes | Story progress unlocks a song outright |
| 7 | Equip two Cards at Loadout Select | Compares your total Power and Defense to the opponent's |
| 8 | Play the Encounter | Your note accuracy becomes your attack roll |

### The Choice That Doesn't Matter

Early on, In Falsus asks whether you want to prioritize the Scenario or challenge the rhythm game. The dialog itself states that the game can be progressed at any difficulty and that the option only affects the initial tutorial. Player experience backs this up — nothing is locked behind either answer, so pick whichever matches your mood.

### Learn These Four Buttons First

Story scenes keep four controls in the top-right corner: step back, auto-play, fast-forward, and the backlog log. The log is the one to remember. In a story where names, schools, and in-world dates pile up quickly, being able to replay a line you clicked past too fast is worth more than any settings tweak.

### Five Beginner Mistakes That Cost the First Encounter

| Mistake | What happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Skipping the Loadout Select tutorial | You enter a fight with empty Phase slots and a 100/100 baseline | Equip a Card in every Phase you can fill |
| Ignoring the Iota Limit | Overload penalty wipes out most or all of your stats | Watch the limit counter before you commit |
| Stacking Iota carelessly | Overlap and Disconnect penalties stack fast | Keep clusters connected and respect the stack rule |
| Playing above your difficulty | Break judgments hand the opponent a 5x critical hit | Start on MIN and move up when you are consistent |
| Never calibrating audio offset | Notes land late no matter how well you play | Calibrate offset before chasing high ranks |

## Difficulties, Note Types, and Grades Explained

In Falsus scales its charts across four difficulty tiers. Community-tracked data from the fan-maintained In Falsus Guide puts the top verified chart level at FBD 15, so there is a long runway above the starting tiers.

| Difficulty | Full name | Keys | What it demands |
|---|---|---|---|
| MIN | Minimal | 4K | Static finger placement, beginner friendly |
| EVO | Evolved | 5K | Active lanes may shift mid-chart |
| ULT | Ultimate | 6K | Agile three-finger movement |
| FBD | Forbidden | 6K+ | Wide notes and the steepest charts in the game |

Every note you meet falls into one of four types, and each one fails differently.

| Note | Input | Failure state |
|---|---|---|
| TAP | A single press on the beat | Standard miss |
| HOLD | Hold the key; no release timing required | Dropping it early |
| FIELD | Keep the mouse cursor inside the purple region | Straying too long briefly kills your input |
| FLICK | Quick mouse flick, left (yellow) or right (green) | Wrong direction or too short a travel |

Scores are graded on a fixed ladder, and the top two tiers are where the real grind lives.

| Grade | Minimum score |
|---|---|
| EX+ | 99,000,000 |
| EX | 98,000,000 |
| AA | 95,000,000 |
| A | 92,000,000 |
| B | 85,000,000 |
| C | 80,000,000 |

### Calibrate Before You Climb

If every note feels slightly late, the problem is usually your setup, not your hands. Community reports point to three fixes: calibrate the in-game Audio Offset, stabilize your frame rate, and clean up mouse or keyboard delay. Keyboard rollover is also a common culprit on 6-key charts — a board that cannot register simultaneous presses will silently eat notes no matter how accurate you are.

## Card Integration Basics: Iota, Blueprints, and Penalties

Card Integration is the part of In Falsus that most players underrate, and it is the section of any In Falsus tutorial that pays off fastest. Each Card has a Blueprint — a hex grid with zones, blank safe tiles, and unsafe tiles. You place Iota into that grid to raise Power and Defense and to unlock Trait slots.

The rules are stricter than they first look. You can stack at most three Iota on a single tile, and every Blueprint has an Iota Limit. Break these and the penalty system starts deleting your stats.

| Penalty | Trigger | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Overload | Exceeding the Iota Limit | Stat multiplier drops |
| Out of Bounds | Placing Iota on an unsafe tile | Stat multiplier drops |
| Overlap | Too many Iota on one tile beyond the stack rule | Stat multiplier drops |
| Disconnect | Iota clusters that are not connected | Stat multiplier drops |

The multiplier ladder is brutal and worth memorizing: zero penalties keeps 100% of your stats, one penalty drops you to 89%, two penalties to 56%, and three or more effectively zeroes your Card out. In practice, a clean, modest layout beats a greedy one almost every time.

Two more rules shape your Encounter setup. Each Phase slot takes exactly one Card, and no two Cards in a battle may share a Blueprint. Any Phase you leave empty fights with a flat 100/100 baseline, which is rarely good enough. Color advantage also matters: the advantaged side gains a 1.25x multiplier to Power and Defense, and community tooling flags those matchups with a triangle in its phase breakdowns.

## Scenario Timeline, Story Unlocks, and the Encounter Loop

The Scenario is In Falsus's story mode, and the Timeline is how you navigate it. A month selector sits on the left, a day ruler runs across the top, and each character gets a colored horizontal line with their portrait. Every node is a scene stamped with a day and a time of day — Morning, Noon, or Afternoon — and anything you have already finished stays available for free re-reading.

The demo's Arc 1, "The First Lie," runs across month 8, days 27–28, and interleaves two point-of-view threads: Mei's classroom storyline and Iris's alley confrontation. It ends the way the full game's arcs do — with a payout in gameplay.

| Story beat | Gameplay unlock |
|---|---|
| Finish the Arc 1 sequence | Song "Deep Into The Vibe" unlocks with difficulty tiers 3, 6, and 10 |
| Confirm the song | LOADOUT button leads straight into the Encounter |
| Enter Loadout Select | Tutorial explains that new Cards come from Scenario progress |
| Play the Encounter | EXACTs deal full damage, NEARs deal half, BREAKs deal none and give the opponent a 5x critical |

That last line is the whole design in miniature: your accuracy is the attack roll. A clean chart turns your Card stats into damage, and a sloppy one hands the fight away regardless of how well you built your Blueprint. The full release expands on this with more arcs, a much larger song list, and the higher FBD charts — see the official In Falsus store page on Steam for current details.

## FAQ

**Is there an in-game In Falsus tutorial for card battles?**
Yes. The Loadout Select screen runs its own tutorial before your first story Encounter, explaining that new Cards are unlocked by progressing through the Scenario. Later, the game notes, you also unlock the ability to improve existing Cards.

**Do I have to finish the story to play rhythm charts?**
No. The Scenario and the rhythm game are separate modes, and the opening choice between them only changes which tutorial you see first. Story progress does unlock songs and Cards, though, so ignoring it entirely will slow your collection down.

**Which difficulty should a total beginner start on?**
Start on MIN. It uses four keys with static finger placement, which lets you learn judgment timing before lane movement and three-finger patterns complicate things. Move to EVO once you can clear MIN charts without Break judgments.

**What is the fastest way to raise my score?**
Three things, in order: calibrate your audio offset, fix any key rollover or input lag issues, then clean up your Blueprint so you carry zero penalties into the fight. A Card at 100% stats with a modest layout will outperform a bloated one at 56% every single time.
