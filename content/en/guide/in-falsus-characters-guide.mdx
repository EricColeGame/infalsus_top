## Why the In Falsus Characters Carry the Entire Story

Most rhythm games hand you a track list and a score screen. In Falsus does something rarer: it hands you five narrators. The game follows five 16-year-old girls through a near-future world where a strange Shroud hangs overhead and, beneath it, no emotion and no lie escapes an unseen gaze. Understanding the **In Falsus characters** isn't trivia — it's the key to how the plot is delivered, because the story is told through their separate perspectives rather than a single hero's journey.

That structure shapes everything. The **In Falsus characters** each occupy a different slice of the same city. Two of them attend the same high school and have never crossed paths. One lives alone and covers her own bills. Another has already made a name for herself as a painter. Their orbits only collide once a mysterious program extends an invitation none of them saw coming — and each girl responds to that invitation in a completely different way.

If you're about to start the game, or you've cleared a few songs and want to know who you're actually looking at on screen, this guide breaks down the full cast: schools, home lives, personalities, voice actors, and how the roster connects to the rhythm systems you'll be grinding.

## Meet the Five Main In Falsus Characters

The official site introduces all five protagonists together, and the lineup is deliberately symmetrical — same age, wildly different lives.

| Character | Age | School | Voice Actor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nia Cana | 16 | Akie High School | Fuka Izumi |
| Mei Alder | 16 | Akie High School | Ran Haruka |
| Ayame Takatsuki | 16 | The Amachi Institute of Art & Design | Kanon Amane |
| Iris Leal | 16 | Oskar Beck High School | Ayame Fujikami |
| Story Étienne | 16 | Xenia Academy | Mirii Saito |

A striking pattern emerges from the official profiles. Nia, Mei, and Iris are each described as friendless. Ayame is the social outlier, surrounded by classmates and contacts. Story's profile pointedly declines to discuss her record or her previous friendships at all. That contrast is intentional — these girls were designed to be isolated in five different ways, and the program recruiting them is the first force that pulls them out of those separate orbits.

Where each girl lives tells you almost as much as her personality. The cast spans two ordinary high schools, one elite arts institute, one academy, and a range of living situations from "parents' apartment" to "fully self-sufficient teenager."

| Ayame Takatsuki | School dorms | Busy social calendar, gallery shows | Charming and careful — possibly too careful |
| Iris Leal | With parents and a younger sister | Frequently absent from school | On thin ice; expulsion is a real risk |
| Story Étienne | School dorms | Transferred from overseas a few years ago | Has chosen not to speak since around the transfer |

That last row is the most intriguing. Story is described as a Mute by choice — a decision she apparently made around the time she moved countries. Her profile also notes she is a voracious reader, which fits a character who observes far more than she says.

## How the Cast Connects to the Rhythm Systems

In Falsus weaves its rhythm gameplay directly into the narrative, so the roster isn't just flavor text. As the protagonists cross paths with others — and sometimes with themselves — those encounters become playable sequences where you brace against incoming notes and a sweeping aerial Field.

| System | What It Does | Why It Ties to the Cast |
|---|---|---|
| Four difficulty tiers | Guides you from first clears to full mastery | Each girl's arc escalates in intensity as the story progresses |
| Iota | Core material collected by clearing songs | The resource layer that rewards continued play |
| Blueprint grids | You slot Iota into grids to produce Cards | Your grid layout changes the resulting Card's effects |
| Cards | Crafted advantages used during songs | Lets you build around how you actually play |
| Scenario mode | Stronger threats tied to story encounters | Rewards expand your Integration options |

The takeaway: how you complete a Blueprint grid determines what your Card does, which means two players following the same character's route can end up with entirely different toolkits. Community reports suggest experimenting with grid layouts early rather than optimizing immediately — the effects are easier to understand once you've seen a few completed patterns.

For the full official breakdown of the cast and systems, the [official In Falsus website](https://infalsus.lowiro.com/) hosts the character profiles and setting details in one place.

## Tips for Following Five Perspectives

Juggling five narrators can be disorienting, especially when two of them share a school. A few habits help:

- **Keep a mental school map.** Akie High School holds Nia and Mei; Amachi Institute holds Ayame; Oskar Beck holds Iris; Xenia Academy holds Story. Four locations, five girls, one shared campus.
- **Track who is friendless versus who is surrounded.** Ayame's social web and Story's deliberate isolation are opposites that frame the middle three.
- **Watch for the "unseen gaze" theme.** Every profile line involves concealment. When a character opens up, that's a story beat, not filler.
- **Revisit profiles after story chapters.** Details like Story choosing silence around her transfer read very differently once you know more.
- **Don't rush the difficulty climb.** The four tiers exist to build flow; skipping ahead to see story content usually costs you more time than it saves.

## FAQ

**How many main In Falsus characters are there?**

Five. Nia Cana, Mei Alder, Ayame Takatsuki, Iris Leal, and Story Étienne are all 16 years old and serve as the game's separate narrators.

**Which of the In Falsus characters do you meet first?**

Based on the recruiter's notes in the profiles, Iris Leal appears to be the first approach. Her profile mentions that she wouldn't be reachable at school and that any misstep could get her expelled before the offer is even made.

**Do all five protagonists play identically?**

No. Because Cards are created by completing Blueprint grids, the effects you generate depend on how you fill each grid. The narrative framing differs per character as well.
