In Falsus asks you to read a 3D rhythm chart with one hand while steering a mouse cursor with the other — and then turns your score into damage through a hex-grid card system. That is a lot to hold in your head at once, which is exactly why a structured In Falsus beginner guide saves you hours of trial and error. Get the setup right and the game teaches you the rest; skip it and you will spend your first week fighting your own hands instead of the chart.

## What Makes In Falsus Different From Other Rhythm Games

In Falsus is a rhythm and card game from lowiro, released on [Steam's store page for In Falsus](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=In+Falsus) on September 9, 2026. The pitch sounds simple — hit notes, build a deck, win fights — but the execution splits your attention in two directions at once.

### Two hands, two completely different jobs

Your keyboard hand owns the six Floor lanes. Your mouse hand owns everything that happens in the air: Field regions and Flick notes. Players who try to survive on keyboard alone hit a wall the moment the first Field note appears, because there is no keyboard equivalent for it.

| Input | What it handles | Common beginner mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keyboard hand | Six Floor lanes, Tap and Hold notes | Never rebinding the cramped default layout |
| Mouse hand | Field regions and Flick notes | Playing keyboard-only until a Field note appears |

### The loop: chart score becomes card damage

Your accuracy on the chart determines how much damage your equipped cards actually deal. A great loadout played sloppily loses to a modest loadout played cleanly. That single idea — accuracy converts stats into damage — is the thread running through everything else in this guide.

## Setup and Rhythm Fundamentals

Five minutes of configuration here prevents hours of frustration later. This is the part of an In Falsus beginner guide that people skip, and it is the part that matters most.

### Rebind before you play a single chart

The default 6K layout is LShift / A / S / D / F / Space, which parks your pinky on LShift for entire songs. Player experience says that cramps fast. Most players move the six lanes to keys their hand already rests on — S / D / F / J / K / L is a popular choice, and A / S / D / F / G / Space works too. The rule is simple: one finger hovers over exactly one lane.

### The difficulty ladder

Start on MIN even if your ego disagrees. MIN is a 4K chart with static finger positions, and it exists specifically so you can learn the game's feel before it starts testing you.

| Difficulty | Full name | Keys | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MIN | Minimal | 4K | Static finger placement, beginner friendly |
| EVO | Evolved | 5K | Active lanes may shift mid-chart |
| ULT | Ultimate | 6K | Requires agile three-finger movement |
| FBD | Forbidden | 6K+ | Full-width notes, the ultimate challenge |

Clear a few MIN charts before touching EVO. Moving from ULT to FBD is not really about note density — it is about wide notes, which only mouse input can clear.

### Know your four note types

| Note | Input | What you actually do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TAP | Keyboard | Standard timed press |
| HOLD | Keyboard | Press the head, hold to the tail — no release timing, so it is free accuracy |
| FIELD | Mouse | Keep the cursor inside the purple region; stray too long and your input briefly dies |
| FLICK | Mouse | Quick directional swipe — yellow means left, green means right, and the swipe must cover the note's full width |

For Field notes, small continuous circles inside the region beat parking the cursor on an edge. For Flicks, a short lazy swipe on a wide note is a BREAK — when yellow or green appears, your mouse hand should already be moving.

### Read the judgement text, not the combo counter

Every note reports EXACT, EXACT LATE/EARLY, or NEAR LATE/EARLY. If you keep seeing LATE, you are hitting behind the beat. That bias readout is the most useful diagnostic tool the game hands you, and it tells you whether to adjust your timing rather than your song choice.

## Card Integration: Blueprints, Iota, and Penalties

This is where most new players lose damage without realizing it. Card Integration takes your rhythm performance and converts it into Power and Defense through a hex-grid puzzle.

### Fill colored Zones first

A Blueprint's colored Zones are the source of your base Power, Defense, and Trait Slots. Blank tiles are safe but grant nothing. On any new Blueprint, cover every colored Zone with matching Iota before you start optimizing anything else.

### Penalties are brutal — read the counter before you craft

| Penalties | Stat multiplier |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 100% |
| 1 | 89% |
| 2 | 56% |
| 3 or more | 0% |

Going from one penalty to two nearly halves your card. A zero-penalty card is always worth more than a slightly stronger card carrying two flags.

| Penalty | Trigger |
| --- | --- |
| Overload | Exceeding the Iota Limit |
| Out of Bounds | Placing Iota on an unsafe tile |
| Overlap | Stacking past the three-per-tile rule |
| Disconnect | Iota clusters not connected to each other |
| Falsehood | Uncapped penalty — check the counter before you commit |

### Hard placement rules worth memorizing

- Iota cannot be rotated or flipped.
- You can stack at most three pieces on a single tile.
- The Iota Limit caps how many pieces you can place at all.
- The finished card must keep both Power and Defense above zero.
- Place your biggest Tier pieces first — they are the ones that will not fit later.

Protect your average Potency, too. Card stats scale with average Potency divided by 100, so one weak filler piece can drag an entire card down further than leaving the tile blank would. If you must choose, leave it empty.

### Build around your character, not around your inventory

Each character's Infusion skill gates which Iota tiers you can use per color, and Core Skills add tolerances that soften placement rules. Community reports note that Nia's "Path to Reality" leans Red while Mei's "4AM" leans Blue, so matching your farmed Iota colors to your character keeps you from carrying a dead inventory.

## Encounters: Phases, Resolve, and Color Advantage

Encounters are where the chart and the cards meet. Five Phases, five card slots, one Resolve race.

### Sequence your five cards

A loadout holds up to five Cards, one per Phase slot, and no two Cards may share a Blueprint. An empty slot fights as a 100 Power / 100 Defense baseline card — fine for early encounters, so do not craft something weak just to fill the fifth slot.

Put your highest-Power card on the Phase where you are most accurate. Damage per note is your Total Power divided by total notes, so accuracy is what actually converts stats into damage.

### Manage Resolve, not health bars

Your Resolve gauge equals your Total Defense. You Connect an encounter by pushing the opponent's Resolve below zero while yours stays above it. Opponent BREAKs deal ×5 crits against you, which means survival is an accuracy check as much as a stat check.

### Exploit the color advantage cycle

Color advantage is one-directional and worth ×1.25 Power and Defense:

| Color | Beats |
| --- | --- |
| Red | Green |
| Green | Purple |
| Purple | Yellow |
| Yellow | Blue |
| Blue | Red |

It will not rescue a bad run, but on borderline encounters it is the difference between a Connect and a retry. Check the encounter's color before locking your loadout.

### Time your Resonance burst

EXACT hits fill the Resonance gauge. A full gauge makes your hits crit for ×5 damage for the rest of that Phase, and the gauge resets every Phase. Since damage per note is fixed, the crit window matters most where the chart is densest — usually the back half. Hold your accuracy through the build-up so the gauge tops off right at the climax.

## FAQ

**Is In Falsus hard for a complete beginner?**
It has a steeper learning curve than most rhythm games because of the keyboard-and-mouse split, but the MIN difficulty exists precisely to ease you in. Follow this In Falsus beginner guide in order and the curve flattens quickly.

**Can I ignore the card system and just play the rhythm charts?**
No. Your chart accuracy feeds damage, but your Cards determine how much damage that accuracy is worth. A clean Blueprint beats a lucky one every time.

**Do I need to fill all five Phase slots before my first encounter?**
Not at all. Empty slots default to a 100/100 baseline card, which is perfectly adequate early on. Wait until you have Iota worth slotting.

**What should I fix first if my scores are stuck?**
Check your judgement text for a LATE or EARLY bias, then calibrate your audio offset. Half of "control problems" turn out to be timing or hardware issues rather than skill.
