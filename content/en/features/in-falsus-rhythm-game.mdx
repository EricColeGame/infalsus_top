## What Is In Falsus? A Rhythm Game Built Around Its Story

## How the Rhythm Gameplay Actually Works

The signature control scheme is mouse and keyboard. Notes arrive as a barrage while a sweeping aerial Field moves around the play area, so reading the screen matters as much as raw reflexes. Progression runs across four difficulties that ramp note intensity and Field movement, guiding players from flow to mastery — the game is explicitly pitched at newcomers and veterans alike.

| Tier | What changes | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Sparser patterns, forgiving timing, gentle Field motion | Learning the control scheme |
| Lower-mid | Denser note groups, faster Field sweeps, longer streams | Players comfortable with two-hand input |
| Upper-mid | Complex rhythms, heavier note volume, tighter accuracy | Regular rhythm game players |
| Top | Maximum density with the aerial Field fully in motion | Score chasers and mastery runs |

*Difficulty tiers above are grouped by player experience; lowiro's marketing copy confirms four difficulties without naming them individually.*

### Starter tips for your first hours

- Treat the first tier as a controls tutorial, not a warm-up you rush past. Mouse-and-keyboard input takes a session or two to feel natural.
- Watch the aerial Field as a whole rather than tracking single notes — the sweep tells you where the next cluster is coming from.
- Clear songs you already enjoy before grinding new ones. Iota collection is tied to clearing, so early variety beats early perfection.
- Resist spending materials the moment you get them. Blueprint layouts are reversible puzzles, but wasted Iota slows your Card growth.
- Keep one flexible loadout and one experimental loadout. Community reports suggest that swapping five-card sets between attempts reveals weaknesses faster than tuning a single deck.

## Iota, Blueprints, and Cards: The Crafting Loop

The In Falsus rhythm game layers a full crafting system on top of its note charts, and it is the feature most likely to surprise players expecting a pure music game. Clearing songs earns Iota, the core material, which you slot into Blueprint grids.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear a song | Unlocks the encounter and its rewards |
| 2 | Collect Iota | The core crafting material |
| 3 | Slot Iota into Blueprint grids | How you fill the grid changes the outcome |
| 4 | Craft Cards | Every card reflects your own arrangement |
| 5 | Equip up to 5 Cards | Alters your approach to difficult songs |
| 6 | Earn further rewards | Expands your Integration possibilities |

Because Blueprint completion shapes a Card's effects, two players with identical materials can end up with very different tools. That is the deckbuilding hook: Blueprints are puzzles, and the grid layout you choose is the answer.

Cards feature artwork of characters you have met and narrative moments you have witnessed, illustrated by a range of artists. If you care about visual collection as much as scoring, this system gives you a second reason to keep clearing content.

## FAQ

**Is In Falsus just a rhythm game, or is there more to it?**
It is a rhythm game first, but the In Falsus rhythm game wraps its song encounters inside a visual-novel narrative and a card-crafting system, so you are managing a story and a deck alongside your accuracy.

**Do I need to be good at rhythm games to enjoy it?**
No. Four difficulties exist precisely so newcomers can start at the gentlest tier, and the crafting system gives you statistical advantages that offset raw execution.

**What control options does it support?**
lowiro highlights a mouse-and-keyboard control system as the signature input. Check the Steam store page for the current list of supported devices before committing.

**How much content is in the base game?**
Over 70 songs — more than 40 of them original — across four difficulties, plus five protagonist routes and the Blueprint and Card systems. Two song packs, one paid and one crossover, extend that list further.

**Is it worth buying the DLC immediately?**
Only if you already enjoy the base soundtrack. The Virtual Singers pack adds five vocal-synth tracks, and the CHUNITHM pack lands October 22 for players who want more variety early.

Whether you come for the note charts or the narrative, the In Falsus rhythm game rewards patience: learn the Field, build your Blueprints deliberately, and the four difficulties will carry you from flow to mastery.
