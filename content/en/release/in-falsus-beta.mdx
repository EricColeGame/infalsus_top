## Beta vs. Launch: What Actually Changes

Comparing a test build to a finished rhythm game is rarely apples to apples, because studios seldom publish a public changelog of test-versus-retail differences. Still, the broad shape of what shifts between the two is consistent enough to plan around.

The biggest change is usually **content volume**. A test build might offer a dozen tracks; the release offers a library, plus downloadable song packs that keep growing after launch. The second change is **system depth** — progression mechanics like card crafting often exist in a simplified form during testing and get their full rules at release.

The third is **stability and note-chart polish**. Chart timing, judgment windows, and the visual clarity of the note field are the things testers complain about most, and they're the things that get refined last. ## Gameplay Systems: Iota, Blueprints, and Cards

In Falsus is not just a note-hitting game. Its rhythm gameplay is woven directly into a narrative structure, and the systems that connect the two are the ones testers usually get to see early.

The loop works like this: you clear songs to collect **Iota**, the core material of the game. You then slot Iota into **Blueprint** grids, and how you complete a grid determines the effects of the **Card** it produces. Those cards give you new advantages and approaches during song gameplay, which matters when the Scenario throws stronger threats at you. Rewards from those encounters expand your Integration possibilities further.

On the input side, expect a barrage of incoming notes and a sweeping aerial **Field**, with four difficulty tiers per song taking you from basic flow to genuine mastery.

| System | What it does | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Iota | Core material earned by clearing songs | Fuels every other progression system |
| Blueprint | Grid you slot Iota into | Grid completion shape determines Card output |
| Cards | Crafted from completed Blueprints | Provide in-song advantages during Scenario |
| Field | Sweeping aerial note presentation | Defines how you read and react to charts |
| Difficulties | Four tiers per song | Gives a clear path from flow to mastery |

If you're coming from other mobile or PC rhythm titles, the adjustment period is mostly about the Blueprint layer. Note-reading transfers quickly; optimizing grid layouts does not.
