The In Falsus trailer leans hard into atmosphere rather than explanation. ## Gameplay: Rhythm First, Everything Else Second

The core loop is straightforward to describe and hard to master. You play songs using a mouse-and-keyboard control scheme, dodging a barrage of incoming notes while a sweeping aerial Field moves around you. Four difficulty tiers carry you from a comfortable flow state up to genuine mastery, and the note density scales accordingly.

Where In Falsus diverges from its genre peers is what happens *between* songs. Cleared tracks drop **Iota**, the game's core crafting material. You slot Iota into **Blueprint** grids — puzzle-like layouts where the arrangement you choose determines the effect of the Card you produce.

| System | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Rhythm gameplay | Note charts across 4 difficulties with a moving aerial Field |
| Iota | Core material collected by clearing songs |
| Blueprints | Grids where material placement shapes a Card's effects |
| Cards | Crafted rewards; equip up to 5 to alter your approach |
| Encounters | Story moments tied to unique songs, fought or assisted |
| Scenario | The narrative mode where stronger threats appear |

Because every Blueprint is effectively a puzzle with multiple valid solutions, two players can clear the same song and walk away with completely different tools. That is the hook — and it is the part an In Falsus trailer can never fully convey, because crafting is a menu-driven, deliberate activity rather than a spectacle.

### Does the Card System Actually Matter?

Community reports suggest yes. The Steam tag list includes both "Deckbuilding" and "Hex Grid," which tells you the Blueprint layouts are hex-based and that optimization is a real pursuit. Cards also feature artwork of characters met and narrative moments witnessed, illustrated by a range of guest artists, so collecting them doubles as a story gallery.

That bureaucratic, faintly sinister voice is a big part of the game's appeal.

## How to Get the Most Out of Your First Few Hours

- **Watch the In Falsus trailer twice.** The first pass sells the mood; the second pass shows you the Field and the note patterns you will actually be reading.
- **Start on the lower two difficulties.** The progression is designed to carry you from flow to mastery, so there is no shame in a slow climb.
- **Do not hoard Iota.** Crafting Cards early gives you tools that make later songs easier.
- **Experiment with Blueprint layouts.** Rearranging the same materials produces different effects, so a failed grid is information, not a loss.
- **Follow one protagonist's route at a time.** Five interlocking perspectives are easier to track when you finish a thread before starting another.
