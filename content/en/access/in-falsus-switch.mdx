## What You Actually Play: Songs, Difficulties, and the Card Layer

In Falsus is not just a note-charting game. It is a rhythm game with a crafting meta wrapped around a visual novel. Songs unlock story beats, story beats unlock materials, and materials become cards that change how you approach harder songs.

The soundtrack is substantial: more than 70 songs at launch, over 40 of them original, drawing heavily on electronic music from Japan's independent scene alongside international artists. That is a bigger original-music commitment than many full-price rhythm titles carry.

| System | What it does | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Iota | Core material collected by clearing songs | The raw resource behind all progression |
| Blueprints | Grids you slot Iota into | How you fill the grid determines the result |
| Cards | Crafted from Blueprints; up to 5 equipped | Reshapes your approach during song gameplay |
| Encounters | Story confrontations tied to specific songs | Narrative and gameplay hit on the same beat |
| Difficulties | Four tiers per song | A guided path from flow to mastery |

Player experience reported on Steam suggests the four-difficulty ladder is generous to newcomers in the early tiers but ramps sharply once the aerial Field and denser note patterns arrive. The mouse-and-keyboard scheme is the official control method, so if you are used to touchscreen rhythm games, expect a short adjustment period.

## The Other Kind of Switch: Five Perspectives, One Story

Here is where the keyword gets genuinely interesting. The "switch" inside In Falsus is a narrative device: you switch between the separate viewpoints of five high school girls living in a near-future world where everyone wears a collar that exposes emotion and lies.

Each protagonist can be played through on her own path, and the routes interlock into a single larger web. That design choice is why the game is tagged as Story Rich and Visual Novel alongside Rhythm.

| Protagonist | School | Voice actor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nia Cana | Akie High School | Fuka Izumi |
| Mei Alder | Akie High School | Ran Haruka |
| Ayame Takatsuki | The Amachi Institute of Art & Design | Kanon Amane |
| Iris Leal | Oskar Beck High School | Ayame Fujikami |
| Story Étienne | Xenia Academy | Mirii Saito |

Community reports consistently single out the perspective switching as the element that keeps the visual novel sections from dragging. Because each girl has a distinct voice, tone, and social world, rotating between them resets your attention in a way a single-protagonist story rarely manages.
